Method and limits

What is measured: what one student is advertised a private bedroom or single-person suite for. Whole-unit prices are excluded because the listing does not establish one student’s share, and the analysis does not divide apartment prices by an assumed number of roommates.

Observation and calculation: one portal listing ID receives one vote. Each listing contributes one advertised midpoint, calculated as (low price + high price) ÷ 2. The Main Campus mean is the sum of the 74 listing midpoints ÷ 74; fixed-price listings have identical low and high values. The article rounds the displayed result to whole dollars.

Limits: this is a descriptive Sept. 9 snapshot of advertisements on NC State’s Off-Campus Housing portal, not a random sample of Raleigh rentals or NC State students. A listing is not a distinct building, tenant, vacant bed or signed lease. The mean is not weighted by vacant beds, bedrooms, residents or enrollment, and advertised prices vary in fee and utility coverage.

Geography
Straight-line distances, band edges and campus overlap

Reference coordinates (latitude, longitude): Main 35.77951, −78.68168; Centennial 35.76870, −78.67750; Centennial Biomedical 35.79847, −78.70408. These are fixed map reference points, not the nearest campus entrance or perimeter. The portal’s own travel-distance field is not used.

  1. Convert latitudes and longitudes from degrees to radians: radians = degrees × π ÷ 180.
  2. Calculate a = sin²(Δlatitude/2) + cos(latitude₁) × cos(latitude₂) × sin²(Δlongitude/2).
  3. Calculate miles = 2 × 3958.7613 × asin(√a). The equivalent atan2 form is used in the website build.
  4. Assign an unrounded distance: d ≤ 1; 1 < d ≤ 3; 3 < d ≤ 5. Never round a distance before deciding its band.

Example: Brook Hill is about 3.000166 miles from the Centennial point, just 0.87 feet outside the three-mile threshold. It belongs to the >3–5 band under these coordinates; geocoding precision can change such edge cases. The workbook shows feet to the nearest 1-, 3- or 5-mile edge and flags listings within 100 feet for inspection. This flag does not imply a measured geocoding error. Straight-line proximity does not establish walking, cycling, bus or driving time.

Earnings and aid comparison
What the aid checkboxes mean

The article starts with wages alone. The two optional checkboxes add NC State’s reported need-based grant average and need-based loan average. They are historical award benchmarks, not a standard FAFSA award or an estimate of a student’s refund.

Assumption: tuition and school fees are covered separately. Checking a box treats the full selected award as available for living expenses. Actual refunds may be smaller. The grant and loan averages describe different recipient groups; selecting both creates a hypothetical combination, not a typical aid package. Loans must be repaid. Food, books, transport and other expenses also compete for these funds.

Source figures

NC State Common Data Set 2025–26, Section H, reports 2024–25 final aid data. H2 K (PDF page 33) gives a $14,743 average need-based scholarship/grant award for full-time undergraduate recipients. H2 M (PDF page 34) gives a $4,106 average need-based loan for its full-time undergraduate recipients, excluding PLUS, unsubsidized and private alternative loans. Neither figure is an average across every NC State student or a 2026–27 award estimate.

NC State’s financial aid disbursement instructions state: “Financial aid must be used first to pay any outstanding tuition and fees, room rent and meal plan charges and textbook charges, if applicable, for each semester.” Only aid exceeding billed charges may be refunded. Dividing an academic-year award by 12 is a common-period comparison for this article, not a monthly disbursement schedule or evidence that the student receives summer aid.

Original NC State excerpt. Financial aid is applied to university charges first. Select the image to open the official NC State source.

Step-by-step calculation

  1. Gross monthly wages: hourly wage × weekly hours × 52 ÷ 12. At 20 hours/week, $15/hour gives $1,300.00/month and $7.25/hour gives $628.33/month. This assumes steady work throughout the year, before taxes.
  2. Grant equivalent, if checked: $14,743 ÷ 12 = $1,228.58/month. Otherwise add $0.
  3. Borrowed funds, if checked: $4,106 ÷ 12 = $342.17/month. Otherwise add $0.
  4. Monthly resources: gross wages + selected grant equivalent + selected borrowed funds. At $15/hour and 20 hours/week with both boxes checked, the total is $2,870.75/month.
  5. Rent share: the selected campus’s unrounded mean room midpoint ÷ monthly resources × 100. The examples below use Main Campus, matching the article’s default. A rounded percentage near 30% is labeled >30% or <30% when needed.

Work-study context

NC State Federal Work-Study lists a general $7.25–$15/hour range, with exceptions for higher pay, and generally advises no more than 20 hours/week. These are published guidelines, not a measurement of what every student worker earns. Actual work-study earnings are limited by the award and funded work period.

The wages above illustrate steady employment at those rates. They do not project year-round FWS funding. Affordability definition
HUD’s household benchmark versus this personal scenario
HUD 30% benchmark 50% severe burden

HUD CHAS background and definitions defines cost burden as monthly housing costs, including utilities, that exceed 30% of monthly income.

For the higher threshold, HUD FY24 ICDBG Frequently Asked Questions — Q29, page 8 notes a housing-cost-greater-than-50% threshold in its IHBG context. HUD CHAS uses the term severe cost burden for monthly housing costs, including utilities, that exceed 50% of monthly income.

This article’s calculation can include a grant and loans, and advertised rents do not consistently include all housing costs. It therefore reports a modeled share of one student’s monthly resources, not a formal HUD household cost-burden classification. The rent measure represents the advertised price for one student’s private bedroom or single-person accommodation.

University benchmarks
Academic-year housing budgets and residence-hall equivalents

NC State’s 2026–27 undergraduate cost of attendance lists housing budgets of $8,738 on campus and $9,808 off campus for full-time fall and spring enrollment. These are budgeting allowances, not measured mean rents, twelve-month quotes or a promise that a student will find housing at that price.

University Housing’s projected 2026–27 rates: double room ($3,970 semester rent + $150 required ResNet) × 2 semesters = $8,240; $8,240 ÷ 9 months = $915.56/month per student. Single room ($4,600 + $150) × 2 = $9,500; $9,500 ÷ 9 = $1,055.56/month. Nine months is the display convention for an academic year, not nine equal monthly bills. Summer is excluded and winter-break arrangements may cost extra. On-campus furnishings, utilities and services differ from off-campus contracts.

Year-to-year room-rate change = (new semester rate − old semester rate) ÷ old semester rate × 100. Example for a double: ($3,970 − $3,800) ÷ $3,800 × 100 = 4.47%. The chart uses room charges alone in both years and excludes ResNet and Coastal Quarters communication fees; the monthly room examples include ResNet. National price context
August 2026 CPI benchmarks, with a fixed release link

The BLS August 2026 CPI release, published September 11, 2026 reports August-to-August increases of 3.1% for lodging while at school, 2.7% for rent of primary residence and 3.4% for all items. The reference uses this fixed August comparison; a rolling “latest release” link would eventually point to a different month.

BLS category definitions: lodging while at school covers college/university owned, leased or controlled housing. Merely advertising a private rental on the NC State referral portal does not put it in that CPI category. Rent of primary residence is the closer national comparison for private off-campus rents. These national twelve-month changes provide context; they do not estimate NC State’s local rent inflation or explain an individual room-rate increase.
