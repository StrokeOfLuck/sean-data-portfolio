Trump’s Iran Rhetoric: Methods and Sources

A descriptive comparison of Trump’s rhetoric around U.S.-involved lethal conflict events in Iran.

Question

How does Donald Trump’s rhetoric differ before, during, and after UCDP-recorded lethal conflict events in Iran with explicit U.S. involvement?

The U.S. involvement focus is deliberate. Joint U.S.–Israel events are kept, along with lethal incidents reported in a U.S. operation context. Records without a documented U.S. role in the reviewed evidence are withheld; this does not prove that the U.S. was uninvolved. This is a descriptive study of language around recorded conflict events. Advance warning is an exploratory hypothesis; the current evidence does not establish a reliable predictive signal.

Data

The October 11, 2026 refresh aligns 49 UCDP Candidate records across 28 event start dates with 90 unique matched posts (132 post/event pairs). All 47 records in the prior extract are retained. The August release adds UCDP 640608, August 30 at Larak Island, whose source headlines explicitly describe a U.S. attack; its best fatality estimate is 2 (low–high 2–3). The selection review also adds UCDP 629301, April 5 in Boyer-Ahmad county, reporting five deaths during the U.S. pilot-rescue operation. July 30 is now shown even though no posts match its windows.

Why 49 records but 28 dates? UCDP can record multiple events with the same start date. The timeline groups those records at one date point, so 49 records produce 28 distinct date points. No records are discarded in this grouping. For example, April 5 now contains the pilot-rescue record and a separate joint U.S.–Israel strike record in Ardebil. Multi-day records use their recorded start date as an anchor.

Post timestamps and post-window membership use America/New_York (EST/EDT). UCDP event dates remain source-coded calendar dates; no intraday event time is available, so a day-before post is not necessarily a full 24-hour lead. Events on adjacent dates can reuse the same post in different windows. Multi-day records appear as context at their start date without assigning their whole fatality total to that day.

The archive ends August 31 at 08:09 EDT. Its partial day-after window is displayed but excluded from the comparison. Empty windows are kept in the timeline and omitted from score averages; an absent post is not a zero score.

UCDP source and selection

What this source is: the Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP) Candidate Events Dataset records lethal organized violence using event dates, locations, actor coding, fatality estimates, and cited source reports. Candidate records are provisional and can be revised.

Why it is used here: UCDP supplies documented event dates around which to group Trump’s posts. The events are selected from UCDP independently of the rhetoric scores. This provides a consistent reference for comparing language before, on, and after recorded conflict events.

Event activity levels

The central event circle and the clickable bottom timeline use the following display categories, based on UCDP fatality estimates and record counts. These thresholds are defined for this visualization; they are not UCDP classifications or tests of statistical significance.

  • Minor: a same-day record below the Notable, High, and Major thresholds.
  • Notable: same-day best estimate ≥ 5, or at least 2 same-day UCDP records.
  • High: same-day best estimate ≥ 10, or at least 3 same-day UCDP records.
  • Major: same-day best death estimate ≥ 25, or same-day civilian deaths ≥ 10.
  • Multi-day Event: a multi-day record begins on that date, but there is no same-day fatality record to assign to that specific date.

The intent is to visually emphasize dates with more substantial same-day UCDP activity, without assigning an entire multi-day fatality total to the first day of a long record.

Approach

  1. Collect Trump’s Truth Social posts from the archived snapshot and UCDP’s Iran-conflict records from the versioned CSV releases. Apply the documented U.S.-involvement selection decisions.
  2. Window the two datasets together: for every distinct UCDP event start date, pull Iran-related posts from the day before, day of, and day after.
  3. Score rhetoric with zero-shot classification. A Hugging Face multi-label zero-shot classifier (facebook/bart-large-mnli) scores each post’s content_text — and only the text, never the UCDP metadata — against six categories: Threat, Victory, Diplomacy, Ceasefire/Ending, Blame Allies, and Media Criticism, each receiving a continuous 0–1 entailment score.
  4. Aggregate means by event start date. Average post scores within each date’s window, then average those date means. Each date with matched posts contributes equally, so a high-volume posting day does not dominate the comparison.
  5. Visualize each event start date at a central node sized and shaded by its activity level, with posts from before, on, and after that date branching around it. Each post also has a small colored badge for its highest-scoring category.

Rhetorical category guide

The six categories are multi-label: the model evaluates each one independently, so a single post can score highly in more than one category. In computational text analysis these are often called rhetorical “frames,” but this page uses category as the plainer label. The values are model entailment scores, not calibrated probabilities.

Results

Open the timeline in its own full-width page ↗ — recommended on desktop, since the embed below is narrower than the page it’s showing and falls back to the mobile layout.

The timeline groups posts around each UCDP event start date and shows their scores for Threat, Victory, Diplomacy, Ceasefire/Ending, Blame Allies, and Media Criticism. A small badge identifies each post’s highest-scoring category. The summary compares average scores across the before, event-day, and after windows, giving each date with posts equal weight. On narrow screens, use the tabs to switch between windows; on wider screens, the three windows appear side by side.

Descriptive findings: Victory framing is prominent across the sampled windows, with mean scores of 0.688 before, 0.769 on the event day, and 0.700 after. Threat scores average 0.558 before, 0.505 on the event day, and 0.485 after. These are model entailment scores, not probabilities of a military event. Each column averages only dates with posts in that window: 21 before, 22 on the event day, and 21 after.

Comparing the same event dates shows how sensitive the apparent day-before Threat increase is to the available sample:

Threat comparison Event dates Before Event day
Posts available before and on the event day 18 0.571 0.503
Posts available in all three windows 14 0.579 0.594

The paired before-minus-event-day difference is +0.068. An exploratory 95% interval from resampling chronological episodes spans approximately −0.166 to +0.352, including no difference. Episodes join event dates no more than three days apart; only eight episodes contribute to this comparison. Requiring all three windows changes the sample and removes the day-before advantage. This sensitivity check does not correct missingness. The evidence supports describing rhetoric around events, while the advance-warning hypothesis remains inconclusive. Inspect the numerical checks.

Exploratory relationship with event severity

The timeline now includes four scatterplots comparing date-level rhetoric with summed UCDP best fatality estimates for records whose start and end dates are the same. Multi-day totals are excluded from this comparison because they cannot be assigned to one day. One dot represents one date with at least one same-day record and a complete, nonempty post window. Post scores are averaged within that window; a date with several posts still contributes one dot.

For the 21 event-day observations, Spearman rank correlations with fatalities are +0.27 for Threat, +0.15 for Victory, +0.05 for Diplomacy, and −0.36 for Ceasefire / Ending. Excluding February 28, the largest-fatality date, gives +0.21, +0.16, +0.13, and −0.34 respectively across 20 dates. The interactive controls also show the before and after windows (20 observations each), so the event-day result is not presented as a general relationship across all windows.

The horizontal axis uses a logarithmic scale to keep smaller events readable alongside the largest date; tick labels show fatality counts. Whiskers show sums of UCDP low and high fatality estimates, not statistical confidence intervals. The timeline’s bubble-size categories also consider record counts and civilian deaths, while these scatterplots isolate total fatalities. The coefficients are descriptive: selected dates, sparse posts, shared posts across nearby windows, and differences between windows limit inference. They do not establish statistical significance, causality, or advance warning. Inspect all plotted values and sensitivity checks.

Further research: testing advance warning

  1. Use only posts available before the outcome date. Keep event-day and day-after language as context, not advance predictors.
  2. Compare the day-before signal with comparable days during the same conflict on which no qualifying U.S.-involved event is recorded. “No UCDP record” is not proof that no operation occurred.
  3. Define the flag and horizon on an earlier development period, then evaluate on later held-out dates. Report false alarms, missed events, precision, recall, and the underlying event rate.
  4. Account for clustered event dates and repeated posts; separate new intervention onsets from follow-on actions during ongoing operations. Sensitivity checks should exclude imprecisely dated and multi-day anchors.
  5. Audit classifier labels by hand. The current Threat description includes both warnings and reports of completed strikes, and some non-threat language can receive a high score. Text-only scores also miss signals carried in attached media.

Limitations

  • Selected sample: 90 unique posts and 28 start dates in one leader’s rhetoric during one conflict. Empty windows are visible but have no rhetoric score. The original post selection was reviewed manually and is preserved, so this is not a fresh exhaustive classification of the full archive.
  • No non-event baseline or held-out warning evaluation yet. This is an exploratory timeline, not an operational prediction tool.
  • Unequal denominators and overlapping windows: before/day-of/after means are not a paired independent sample. Several dates can share the same post; a previous-day post may be reacting to an earlier strike.
  • Provisional event timing and attribution: UCDP Candidate records can be revised. Source-coded dates lack intraday timing; shared dyad coding alone does not identify the attacker.
  • Event-selection coverage: the current review checks supplied headline/source fields for all 80 Iran-conflict records, with two full AFP reports additionally checked. The 31 withheld records are not proven Israel-only. Further source review could establish U.S. involvement and expand the sample. The rescue record documents U.S. operation context with the strike actor unspecified.
  • Fatality-based inclusion: the event set does not cover every military operation, diplomatic moment, sanction, or negotiation.
  • Model scores: BART entailment scores are uncalibrated and heuristic, not probabilities of intervention or validated measures of intent. The classifier is not fine-tuned for this domain.
  • Snapshot coverage: August 31 is incomplete in the post archive, and later UCDP months are not part of the latest listed August release.
