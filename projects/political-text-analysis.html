Political Text Analysis

Computational analysis of political rhetoric around real-world events.

Question

How does Donald Trump’s Iran-related Truth Social rhetoric change in the day before, day of, and day after UCDP-recorded U.S. or joint U.S.–Israel conflict events, and is threat language elevated beforehand?

Data

Two sources are aligned by date:

  • Trump’s Truth Social posts. The full public archive of his original posts (not reblogs) is pulled from a Hugging Face dataset, filtered to calendar-year 2026 and to posts mentioning Iran (401 posts YTD as of this snapshot).
  • UCDP conflict events. The Uppsala Conflict Data Program’s Georeferenced Event Dataset, filtered to events with US or joint US–Israel involvement related to the Iran conflict (through July 2026). UCDP is manually researcher-coded from primary reporting on organized violence, which makes it a more reliable “ground truth” for event timing and severity than automated event trackers like GDELT, though its inclusion rule (battle-related fatalities) means it can miss purely diplomatic or political moments that don’t involve violence.

For each UCDP event, a 1-day event window (day before / day of / day after, in Eastern time) is built around it, and any Iran-related Trump posts falling in that window are attached. This produces 393 unique event-dates with matched posts, spanning 543 underlying UCDP event records.

Each event-date is also assigned a significance level (Standard, Notable, High, Major, or Context) from same-day UCDP record count, best fatality estimate, and civilian death count — e.g., 2026-02-28 and 2026-04-06 are both coded “Major” based on same-day death tolls in the dozens to low hundreds. The exact rule is below.

Significance levels

The central event circle and the clickable bottom timeline in the visualization are both sized and colored by this classification, derived directly from the UCDP event workbook:

  • Minor: another same-day UCDP event date.
  • Notable: same-day best estimate ≥ 5, or at least 2 same-day UCDP records.
  • High: same-day best estimate ≥ 10, or at least 3 same-day UCDP records.
  • Major: same-day best death estimate ≥ 25, or same-day civilian deaths ≥ 10.
  • Multi-day Event: a multi-day record begins on that date, but there is no same-day fatality record to assign to that specific date.

The intent is to visually emphasize dates with more substantial same-day UCDP activity, without assigning an entire multi-day fatality total to the first day of a long record.

Approach

  1. Collect Trump’s Truth Social posts (Hugging Face archive) and UCDP’s Iran-conflict event records (filtered Excel export), both refreshed periodically as new data becomes available.
  2. Window the two datasets together: for every UCDP event, pull Iran-related posts from the day before, day of, and day after.
  3. Score rhetoric with zero-shot classification. A Hugging Face multi-label zero-shot classifier (facebook/bart-large-mnli) scores each post’s content_text — and only the text, never the UCDP metadata — against six labels: Threat, Victory, Diplomacy, Ceasefire/Ending, Blame Allies, and Media Criticism, each a continuous 0–1 probability.
  4. Aggregate event-balanced means. Because posting volume varies a lot day to day, scores are first averaged within each event, then averaged across events — so a single high-volume posting day doesn’t dominate the pattern.
  5. Visualize the result as a connected, per-event timeline: each UCDP event sits at a central node sized/colored by significance, with the day-before/day-of/day-after posts and their six rhetorical scores branching around it. Each post is also tagged with its single dominant frame as a small colored badge.

Results

The interactive timeline above shows, for each UCDP event, the surrounding Trump posts and their per-post Threat/Victory/Diplomacy/Ceasefire/Blame Allies/Media Criticism scores (plus a small badge showing that post’s single dominant frame), and a summary comparison of event-balanced averages across the day-before/day-of/day-after windows. The timeline itself is responsive: on wide viewports (like the standalone page above) it shows the full connected rail with day-before/day-of/day-after branches side by side; on narrow viewports (including this embedded version, since its column is narrower than the 900px breakpoint) it collapses into a tab switcher instead.

The clearest evidence for telegraphing: average Threat language is actually higher the day before a UCDP event (0.55) than on the event day itself (0.52) or the day after (0.48). It’s also visible in individual posts — in the day before the April 6 event (34 killed in strikes on Iran’s Guards intelligence chief and a university), Trump wrote: “Tuesday will be Power Plant Day, and Bridge Day, all wrapped up in one, in Iran. There will be nothing like it!!!” — naming the target categories before further strikes happened.

Blame Allies and Media Criticism were added as later passes after the first four labels. Blame Allies (criticizing NATO/European allies for not helping) is low overall but ticks up on the event day (0.13) versus the day before (0.07) and day after (0.11). Media Criticism (attacking press coverage as biased or fake) varies a lot post-to-post (from near 0 to near 1) but doesn’t move much across the three windows (0.34–0.35 throughout) — a real, variable signal at the post level that just isn’t specifically tied to event timing. (A different sixth label, Self-Credit, was tried and dropped earlier — it scored near-ceiling on almost every post regardless of timing, which made it more a constant feature of the voice than a useful signal.)

Limitations

  • Small sample. 87 unique posts are scored across the 26 events shown here (many event-windows have no matching Trump post at all), and results should be read as a descriptive pattern within one leader’s rhetoric during one specific conflict, not a generalizable claim.
  • Zero-shot labels are heuristic. The classifier was never fine-tuned on this domain; label probabilities reflect a general-purpose model’s association between text and label names, not a validated measure of intent or sentiment.
  • UCDP’s fatality-based inclusion criterion means the event set may under-represent diplomatically significant moments (sanctions, negotiation breakdowns) that didn’t involve violence — so “no nearby UCDP event” doesn’t mean “nothing happened.”
  • No counterfactual baseline. This only looks at posts already inside event windows — it doesn’t compare day-before Threat language ahead of strikes against day-before Threat language on dates where no strike ultimately happened. Without that comparison, elevated day-before Threat could reflect genuine advance signaling, or it could just be Trump’s ordinary baseline level of threatening rhetoric during an active conflict, which happens to precede strikes only because strikes were frequent during this period.
  • Both the Truth Social archive and the UCDP extract are periodic snapshots (current through mid-2026), not a live feed; this page will be refreshed as new data comes in this semester.
